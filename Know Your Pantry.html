What Is Honey Powder? Everything You Need To Know

By The KnowYourPantry Team / November 3, 2024

Honey is a beloved natural sweetener that has been used for centuries in cooking and baking. But have you heard of honey powder?

This dehydrated form of honey has been gaining popularity in recent years, thanks to its versatility and long shelf life.

In this article, we’ll explore everything you need to know about honey powder – from how it’s made to its health benefits and culinary uses.

So grab a cup of tea and let’s dive into the world of honey powder!

What Is Honey Powder?

Honey powder is a dehydrated form of natural honey that typically contains less than 2 percent of the typical liquid content of the traditional, liquid form of this golden sweetener. It is made by dehydrating real honey until all the moisture has evaporated. From there, it’s ground into a fine powder and often mixed with stabilizers like starch or sugar to keep it in fluffy dust form.

Unlike real honey, honey powder dissolves evenly when mixed and also doesn’t provide the honey texture. The consistency is much easier to maintain in powder form, making it perfect for dry rubs and cake, cookie, or bread mixes. It is also a great sugar substitute because it is a natural sugar that has a lower glycemic index than traditional white sugar. In baked goods, it creates sweetness while also providing the same type of consistency as white sugar. You can use it cup for cup as a substitute.

Honey powder has a long shelf life, making it especially great for long-term storage. It provides the typical flavor characteristics of honey, but without the water and can last for decades if stored properly.

What Is Honey Powder And How Is It Made?

Honey powder is made by dehydrating real honey until all the moisture has evaporated. The process starts by spreading honey thinly on a piece of parchment paper or fruit puree sheet for the dehydrator. It is essential to spread it evenly, so it dehydrates at the same rate throughout. Keeping it in a thin layer cuts down on the time it takes and helps prevent issues during the process.

Once the honey is spread out, it is placed in a dehydrator or oven to dry. The temperature should be set at a low heat, around 110-120°F, to prevent overheating and burning. It can take several hours for the honey to dry completely, depending on the thickness of the layer and the temperature used.

Once the honey is dehydrated, it is ground into a fine powder using a food processor or blender. Some manufacturers may add stabilizers like starch or sugar to keep it in fluffy dust form. However, it is essential to check the ingredients list before purchasing commercial honey powder to avoid any additives that may not be desirable.

The Benefits Of Using Honey Powder

There are several benefits to using honey powder over traditional liquid honey. Firstly, honey powder is a hassle-free option. Dehydrated honey is easier to use than liquid honey, which can get sticky. It dissolves evenly when mixed and also doesn’t provide the honey texture, making it easier to maintain consistency in recipes.

Secondly, honey powder is a great sugar substitute. It is a natural sugar that has a lower glycemic index than traditional white sugar, making it a healthier alternative. In baked goods, it creates sweetness while also providing the same type of consistency as white sugar. You can use it cup for cup as a substitute.

Thirdly, powdered honey is an affordable natural sweetener. Many other sugar/sweet substitutes can have a pretty high price tag compared to honey powder, so it is a good value.

Fourthly, honey powder has antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties that will actively defend your skin against acne and future breakouts. Honey Powder contains large amounts of Vitamins B and C, as well as essential minerals like calcium, iron, zinc, potassium, phosphorous, magnesium, and selenium. All of which will help control surface oils and strengthen your skin’s foundation.

Lastly, since it is powdered in form, its addition to the dish is more accurately handled as compared to raw honey. Moreover, it extends the list of meals in which honey can be added for flavoring. It can be used as a sprinkle on cereal, puddings, biscuits, cakes, and breads. It has the benefit of non-stickiness, mess-free application, no crystallization issues, and easy clean up.

Culinary Uses For Honey Powder

Honey powder is a versatile ingredient that can be used in a variety of culinary applications. Its sweet, honey flavor is perfect for enhancing the taste of baked goods, beverages, and even savory dishes. Here are some culinary uses for honey powder:

1. Baked Goods: Honey powder is an excellent substitute for liquid honey in baked goods such as cookie, bread, or cake mixes. It can add moisture to your baked goods and keep them fresh and moist for longer. Additionally, it can even be a solution to a cake that usually turns out dry.

2. Sweetening Beverages: Honey powder can be used as a natural sweetener in both cold and hot beverages like tea, coffee, lemonade, and fruit drinks. It dissolves evenly when mixed and provides a sweet flavor without the sticky texture of liquid honey.

3. Dry Rubs: Honey powder is perfect for dry rubs used on meat or vegetables. It adds a sweet and savory flavor to your dishes without making them too sticky.

4. Cereal and Oatmeal: Sprinkle honey powder on your cereal or oatmeal for a sweet, delicious taste.

5. Savory Dishes: Honey powder can be used in place of sugar in savory dishes like BBQ rubs, spaghetti sauce, or ketchup. It adds a touch of sweetness while still maintaining the savory flavor profile.

6. Beauty Products: Honey powder can also be used in cosmetics for manufacturing different bath and beauty products including creams, lotions, soaps, scrubs, facials, masks, body wraps, bath teas, milk baths, bath salts and bath bombs. Honey is one of the oldest skin care ingredients on this Earth. It is said to be a natural skin cleanser and softener.

How To Substitute Honey Powder In Recipes

If you’re looking to use honey powder in your recipes, it’s important to know how to substitute it for liquid honey or other sweeteners. Here are some tips:

1. Cup for cup substitution: Honey powder can be used as a direct substitute for sugar in recipes. Simply use the same amount of honey powder as you would sugar.

2. Reconstituting: If you want to use honey powder as a liquid sweetener, simply mix 1/4 cup cold water to 1 cup of honey powder to create liquid honey.

3. Baked goods: Honey powder works well in baked goods such as cakes, cookies, and breads. It creates sweetness while also providing the same type of consistency as white sugar.

4. Dry rubs: Honey powder is perfect for dry rubs, as it creates a sweet and savory flavor without adding any moisture.

5. Hot and cold beverages: Honey powder can be added to hot or cold beverages such as tea or lemonade for a touch of sweetness.

6. Sprinkling: You can sprinkle honey powder over cereal, yogurt, or ice cream for a sweet and delicious taste.

Remember that honey powder does not retain the original flavor of honey but retains some of its qualities. It is also an excellent substitute for sugar and has a longer shelf life than liquid honey. With these tips, you can easily incorporate honey powder into your favorite recipes and enjoy its many benefits.

Where To Buy Honey Powder And How To Store It

If you’re interested in trying honey powder, you can find it at specialty food stores, health food stores, and online retailers. It’s important to note that not all honey powder is created equal. Some brands may contain additives or fillers, so be sure to read the label carefully before making a purchase. Look for brands that use 100% pure honey and avoid those that contain added sugars or artificial flavors.

When storing honey powder, it’s important to keep it in an airtight container in a cool, dry place. Exposure to heat and moisture can cause the powder to clump and lose its flavor. A glass jar with a tight-fitting lid is an ideal storage option. If stored properly, honey powder can last for up to two years without losing its flavor or quality.

Potential Drawbacks And Precautions When Using Honey Powder

While honey powder is generally safe for consumption, there are a few potential drawbacks and precautions to keep in mind. Firstly, it’s important to note that honey powder made from the nectar of rhododendrons can be toxic and may cause heart problems, low blood pressure, and chest pain. Therefore, it’s important to ensure that the honey used to make the powder is from a safe source.

Additionally, using large amounts of honey powder might increase blood sugar levels in people with type 2 diabetes. Honey contains sugar and should be used in moderation. It’s also important to note that individuals with pollen allergies should avoid honey powder as it may cause allergic reactions.

When using honey powder in recipes, it’s important to keep in mind that it may not provide the exact same texture as liquid honey. While it can be a great substitute for dry dishes, it may not work as well in recipes that require the liquid consistency of traditional honey.

Lastly, while honey powder has a long shelf life, it’s important to store it properly to ensure its longevity. It should be kept in an airtight container in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight.
