Seeing Lyme Regis, Between the Jurassic and Jane Austen

Because of the Jane Austen connection, Lyme Regis was on our itinerary for our southern England trip. However this small city has a lot of seaside charm and a lovely beach, and it’s definitely worth the time to stop and stay. We both really enjoyed our time in the “Pearl of Dorset.”

A Short History of Lyme Regis


Artwork by Dmitry Bogdanov

I normally start my histories a few thousand years back, if not a few hundred. But the history of Lyme Regis that a lot of people know of is actually the prehistoric and dates back a few million years to the Jurassic period. So it’s quite a unique place!

Remnants of the Triassic to Cretaceous periods are quite visible in the cliffs and rocks. There are tons and tons of Ammonites but Lyme Regis is also known for the Ichthyosaur and Plesiosaurus (my personal favorite).

In human history, the town as we know it became a more prominent port city in the 13th century. Despite its Tudor history, there’s only one Tudor building still standing today, and it’s been redesigned to look like a Georgian building. During the Georgian era, there was more of a shift to tourism and coming to Lyme Regis was typically done for getting the good seaside air.

Jane Austen visited Lyme Regis on several occasions and it became the setting for Louisa Musgrove’s infamous fall in Persuasion, so it’s also considered a “Jane Austen site.”

In the early 19th century, Mary Anning discovered the Ichthyosaur here when she was only a preteen and she’d grow to be one of the country’s most prominent paleontologists. She made many discoveries of previously unknown dinosaurs and ran a fossil business. She’s one of the most celebrated names of Lyme Regis today, if not the most.

Visiting Lyme Regis Today

Of course, a huge draw of Lyme Regis is the Cobb and the beaches nearby. The Cobb is the stone wall that encompasses the harbor. It’s a nice little walk to get some good views of the town and there’s also a small aquarium to see at the farther end. It’s also in this area you can get a boat trip out with one of the many companies there. We personally didn’t take a boat trip here so I don’t have any recommendations.

The Cobb is name dropped in Persuasion and there are several steps that look a bit dangerous to jump off of. If you’re interested in Jane Austen, you’ll also want to head by the Pyne House, which is on Broad Street. It’s marked with a blue plaque and is said to be where Jane stayed on at least one of her trips to Lyme Regis. However it’s not open to the public.

If you want to go to the beach, there’s lots of room. The Front Beach is the rocky beach along the main promenade and closer to the Cobb is the Sandy Beach which is, you guessed it, sandy. Here you can also rent a kayak or paddleboard.

If you’d like, you can also rent a colorful beach hut for the day or the week. It’s actually a lot cheaper than I’d have anticipated but as we didn’t book one ourselves, we’re not sure how competitive it is to secure a reservation.

Across the Cobb is Monmouth Beach which is a lot less crowded and more of a rocky beach.

One thing we didn’t get to do is visit the Lyme Regis Fossil Beach which is a short walk from the town center. It’s a popular place for people to go searching for fossils. There’s lots of coastline that’s not super suitable for setting up your beach chairs but makes for better sealife and fossil searching.

The seagulls in Lyme Regis are notoriously aggressive and I saw it myself first hand. So keep your guards up when you’re walking around with fries or an ice cream cone or you could end up a victim of these ruthless birds.

We went to the Lyme Regis Museum and loved it. It’s a small museum but it’s packed with information about the history of Lyme Regis, its literary connections, and of course fossils and information on the dinosaurs that used to live here in a very different time. They also host a selection of walks if there’s a specific part of history you’d like to learn more about.

Lyme Regis also houses the Dinosaurland Fossil Museum but we didn’t visit since we got our fix at the first museum. But it’s something to keep in mind if you’re more invested in dinosaurs.

You will see around town that there’s a lot of homage to their Jurassic heritage.

A cute place to visit is The Mill which is a big tucked away. It’s the old mill of the town and you can see how flour was processed there in their small museum. However it’s also an area where there are a few independent shops, some restaurants, and art galleries. It’s worth keeping on your list so you know to pop by.

There’s a big hill down to the main part of Lyme Regis so if you’re walking, you’ll probably be doing a bit of a climb. It’s also quite steep and I can only imagine how it would’ve been in Jane Austen’s day with the horses and carriages running down, ha.

Also note that the buses don’t run in the evening. We walked down to town for dinner once and planned to take a bus back up, only to find out they had stopped around 5:00pm-ish. So we got to take the big trek up to our lodge (again). Just something to keep in mind.

Also, you’ll notice there are lots of places to buy fossils. If that’s something that interests you, I’d recommend doing some looking around before purchasing. My mom bought the fist fossils we saw and the ones she paid $20-35 for cost only $5 in certain stores. And I can almost guarantee you that the type of fossil you see will probably be on sale somewhere else.

As we were just sight-seeing, we spent about a day and a half in Lyme Regis. It’s not a super large place and we were able to see what we wanted and had lots of time to relax. But obviously, if you’re looking for a nice beach escape, you can definitely spend more time there. Maybe make time for kayaking, a boat tour, the arcade, a dinosaur fossil tour, and trying a bunch of different restaurants. It can make a lovely few days’ trip.

We went to summer and I have to say, as it is a beach town, it’s definitely more crowded but probably also more charming. My mom doesn’t like water stuff but I think it would’ve been more enjoyable had we taken advantage of the sea activities there.

I definitely recommend a trip to Lyme Regis. It’s a lovely place to visit for the history, the charm, and the seaside activities – so if that’s what you’re looking for on your southern England vacation, then Lyme Regis should definitely be on your list.
